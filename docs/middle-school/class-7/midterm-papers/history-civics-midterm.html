🏛️ Class 7 History & Civics: Midterm Question Papers & Solutions

This section compiles solved question papers and model answers for the Class 7 First Terminal Examination (FTE) and unit tests.

📄 1. Class 7 First Term Examination (Paper 2025)​

Time Allowed: 2 Hours | Maximum Marks: 80
Answers to this Paper must be written on the paper provided separately.

Section A: Civics (30 Marks)​

Question 1: Multiple Choice & Short Objective (10 Marks)​

  1. The Constitution of India was adopted on:
    • A) 15th August 1947
    • B) 26th November 1949
    • C) 26th January 1950
    • D) 30th January 1948
      Answer: B) 26th November 1949
  2. The Directive Principles of State Policy are borrowed from the constitution of:
    • A) USA
    • B) UK
    • C) Ireland
    • D) France
      Answer: C) Ireland
  3. Define the term 'Sovereign' as stated in the Preamble. (2 Marks)
    Answer: It means India is internally supreme and externally free from any outside control [1 Mark]. It has the power to legislate on any subject [1 Mark].

Question 2: Structured Civics Questions (20 Marks)​

  1. State any two reasons why our Constitution is considered the supreme law of the land. (3 Marks)
  2. Differentiate between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles with two points each. (4 Marks)
  3. Explain the significance of the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. (3 Marks)

Section B: History (50 Marks)​

Question 3: The Delhi Sultanate (25 Marks)​

Theme: Ala-ud-din Khalji’s military campaigns and economic controls, and
Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq’s ambitious reforms.

Student NAME: __________________________ Max Marks: 45

Section A: Brief Answers

   1.​ Name the Delhi ruler who followed the 'Blood and Iron' policy.
   2.​ Which ruler took steps to control market prices?
   3.​ Define the term 'Ulemas'.
   4.​ Name the ruler who built 100 'sarais' (rest houses) for travelers and maintained
       1,80,000 slaves.

Section B: Detailed Analysis

   1.​ What specific measures did Ala-ud-din Khalji take to keep the prices of commodities
       under control?
   2.​ Ala-ud-din wanted to become a "Second Alexander". List the specific steps he took to
       achieve this:
           ○​ Expansion strategy
           ○​ Control over nobles' wealth
           ○​ Social restrictions on nobles
   3.​ Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq’s reign is known for ambitious schemes. Explain the failure of:
           ○​ (i) Taxation in the Doab.
           ○​ (ii) Transfer of the Capital.

Section C: Multiple Choice

   1.​ He fixed the prices of food grains and cloth rigorously:
           ○​ (a) Ala-ud-din Khalji (b) Babur (c) Akbar (d) Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq
   2.​ He transferred the capital from Delhi to Devagiri:
           ○​ (a) Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq (b) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (c) Babur (d) Muhammad
               Ghori
   3.​ He built many towns like Ferozepur, Jaunpur, and Hissar:
           ○​ (a) Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq (b) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (c) Babur (d) Muhammad
               Ghori
   4.​ Arrange in chronological order: (i) Sayyid dynasty, (ii) Slave dynasty, (iii) Battle of
       Panipat, (iv) Invasion of Timur.
           ○​ (a) i, iii, ii, iv (b) iv, i, ii, iii (c) ii, i, iv, iii (d) iii, ii, iv, i

Section D: Fill in the Blanks

   1.​ The __________ dynasty was replaced by the Tughlaq dynasty.
   2.​ __________ was the founder of the Tughlaq dynasty.
   3.​ __________ decided to shift his capital from Delhi to __________.

Section F: Skill-Based Questions

   1.​ Identify the Ruler (Task A):
          ○​ His General Malik Kafur defeated the rulers of Devagiri and Warrangal.
          ○​ He constructed the royal tank known as 'Hauz Khas' at Delhi.
   2.​ Identify the Ruler (Task B):
          ○​ He transferred his capital from Delhi to Devagiri (Daulatabad).
          ○​ He introduced a controversial token currency system.

Paper 1: Solutions

   ●​ Section A: 1. 1206 CE–1526 CE (ruling from Delhi). 2. Qutb-ud-din Aibak. 3. (i) No
      hereditary claim to throne; (ii) Nobles wouldn't accept a slave; (iii) Hindu princes took
      advantage of confusion. 4. Razia Sultana.
   ●​ Section B:
           1.​ Iltutmish. Historical Significance: His survival was vital because he successfully
               transitioned the Sultanate from a military occupation into a permanent state.
           2.​ Blood and Iron: Use of ruthless force/military power. He eliminated "The Forty"
               and forced nobles to perform Sijdah. Historical Significance: This restored the
               fear and prestige of the Sultan, which had been lost under weak successors.
   ●​ Section C: 1(c); 2(d); 3(d).
   ●​ Section D: Qutb-ud-din Aibak; Iltutmish.
   ●​ Section E: 1. Slave Dynasty. 2. Powerful Turkish officials/chiefs. 3. Introduced the Tanka
      (silver coin) and saved India from the Mongols (Chengiz Khan). 4. They were orthodox
      and did not like being ruled by a woman.
   ●​ Section F: (a) Delhi; (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak; (c) Iltutmish.

Paper 2: Solutions

   ●​ Section A: 1. Balban; 2. Ala-ud-din Khalji; 3. Orthodox scholars of Islamic learning; 4.
      Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
   ●​ Section B:
          1.​ Registration of merchants, checking black marketing, and fixing prices for
              grains/cloth. Historical Significance: These controls allowed him to maintain a
              massive standing army at a low cost to defend against Mongol invasions.
          2.​ (1) Expansion from Himalayas to Madurai; (2) Reduced nobles' wealth; (3)
              Banned social parties/marriages without permission. Historical Significance: By
              weakening the nobles, he ensured they were too poor and isolated to rebel.
          3.​ (i) Taxation: Famine made collection impossible; peasants fled. (ii) Capital
              Transfer: The 1500km journey killed many; Delhi was eventually found to be a
              better base to guard the frontier. Historical Significance:
              Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq was a visionary whose failures were caused by poor
              timing and execution rather than lack of intelligence.
   ●​ Section C: 1(a); 2(a); 3(b); 4(c - see correction above).
   ●​ Section D: 1. Khalji; 2. Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq; 3. Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq, Devagiri
      (Daulatabad).
   ●​ Section E: 1-(c); 2-(a); 3-(b).
   ●​ Section F: 1. Ala-ud-din Khalji; 2. Mohammad-bin-Tughlaq.

Paper 3: Solutions

   ●​ Section A: 1. A conqueror from Central Asia (invaded 1398). 2. Bahlol Lodhi. 3. Ibrahim
      Lodhi. 4. Babur.
●​ Section B: Relations between Ibrahim Lodhi and his Afghan nobles soured. The
   discontented chiefs invited Babur from Kabul. Historical Significance: This internal
   disunity among the ruling class paved the way for the 300-year Mughal era.
●​ Section C: 1(a); 2(a); 3(b).
●​ Section D: 1. Firoz Shah Tughlaq; 2. 1526, Babur and Ibrahim Lodhi.
●​ Section E: 1-(a); 2-(b).
●​ Section F: 1. Ibrahim Lodhi; 2. Iltutmish.
