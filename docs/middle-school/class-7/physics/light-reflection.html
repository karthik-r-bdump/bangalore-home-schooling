                                             LIGHT
                                   Class 7 Physics • Short Notes

 Big idea: We see objects when light from them - produced or reflected - enters our eyes.




1. Reflection of Light
  Reflection is the bouncing back of light into the same medium when it strikes a smooth or
     polished surface.
  A ray of light is a straight line with an arrow showing the direction in which light travels.

Types of reflection
     Regular / specular reflection                        Diffuse / irregular reflection
     Occurs on a smooth surface. Parallel incident        Occurs on a rough surface. Reflected rays
     rays remain parallel after reflection and a          spread in different directions; this allows us to
     clear image is formed.                               see ordinary objects.



2. Terms Connected with Reflection
Incident ray: the ray that strikes the reflecting surface.
Reflected ray: the ray that bounces back into the same medium.
Point of incidence: the point where the incident ray strikes the surface.
Normal: a line drawn perpendicular (90°) to the surface at the point of incidence.
Angle of incidence, i: the angle between the incident ray and the normal.
Angle of reflection, r: the angle between the reflected ray and the normal.

 Remember: Angles of incidence and reflection are always measured from the normal, not from the
 mirror.




3. Laws of Reflection
  First law: The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection: ∠i = ∠r.
  Second law: The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in
     the same plane.
  These laws apply to every reflecting surface, whatever its shape, size or nature.
  For normal incidence, i = 0° and r = 0°; the ray retraces its path.

4. Plane Mirror
  A plane mirror is a flat, smooth reflecting surface.
  It forms an image because reflected rays either meet, or appear to meet, at a point.

Image of a point object
  Rays from object O reflect from the mirror and diverge. Their backward extensions appear to
    meet at I behind the mirror.
  The image is virtual: reflected rays do not actually pass through I, so the image cannot be
    caught on a screen.
  Object distance u equals image distance v: u = v.




                      Figure 4.4: Image of a point object formed by a plane mirror

Image of an extended object
     Locate the image of each point of the object using reflected rays and their backward
        extensions.
     For object OP, the image is IP′. It is upright and the same height as the object: h′ = h.




                        Figure 4.5: Geometric construction of the image of an extended object


5. Characteristics of the Image in a Plane Mirror
     Virtual: cannot be obtained on a screen.
     Erect: upright, not upside down.
     Same size: image height equals object height, h′ = h.
     Equal distance: image distance behind the mirror equals object distance in front, v = u.
     Laterally inverted: left and right sides appear interchanged.

    Lateral inversion: If you raise your left hand, the image appears to raise its right hand. Top and bottom
    are not interchanged.




6. Real and Virtual Images
              Feature                       Real image                                    Virtual image


       Rays                    actually meet                               only appear to meet


       Screen                  can be obtained                             cannot be obtained


       Orientation             usually inverted                            erect


       Intersection            actual                                      imaginary backward extensions
