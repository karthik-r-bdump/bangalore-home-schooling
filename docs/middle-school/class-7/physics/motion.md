# 🚗 Motion

## 📐 Key Definitions & Equations
- **Speed**: Distance covered per unit time ($s = \frac{d}{t}$).
- **Velocity**: Speed in a specified direction.
- **Acceleration**: Rate of change of velocity ($a = \frac{v - u}{t}$).

1. Rest, Motion and Reference Point
    REST
    A body is at rest if its position does not change with time with respect to a chosen reference point or its
    surroundings.


    MOTION
    A body is in motion if its position changes with time with respect to a chosen reference point or its
    surroundings.



Why is a reference point necessary?
Rest and motion are relative terms. The same object may be at rest for one observer and moving for
another.

     A student sitting in a moving bus is at rest relative to the bus.
     The same student is moving relative to a person standing beside the road.
     A fixed point chosen to describe position or motion is called a reference point or origin.
    To describe motion clearly, keep three things in mind
    1. Distance of the body from the reference point/origin.
    2. Direction of motion.
    3. Time of motion.



2. Types of Motion
2.1 Translatory Motion
In translatory motion, all parts of a body move through the same distance in the same interval of
time.

                  Type                                    Path                                  Examples
    Rectilinear motion                   Straight line                             Car on a straight road; freely falling
                                                                                   stone
    Curvilinear motion                   Curved path                               Thrown ball; javelin in flight

2.2 Rotatory Motion
     A body has rotatory motion when it rotates about a fixed axis.
     Each point of the body moves in a circular path around the axis.
     Examples: potter's wheel, spinning top, rotating fan, Earth rotating about its axis.

2.3 Oscillatory Motion
     Oscillatory motion is the repeated to-and-fro motion of the whole body about a mean position.
     Examples: simple pendulum, a child on a swing, piston moving to and fro.

2.4 Circular Motion
     Circular motion is motion along the circumference of a circle.
     The direction of motion changes continuously.
     Examples: stone tied to a string and whirled, satellite around Earth, hands of a clock.
Rotatory motion vs Circular motion
                       Rotatory Motion                                                Circular Motion
    Body rotates about a fixed axis passing through the body.    Body travels along a circular path about a centre/axis.
    Example: spinning top.                                       Example: stone whirled on a string.
    A point on the body traces a circle around the axis.         The moving body itself goes around the circular path.

2.5 Vibratory Motion
     Vibratory motion occurs when a part of a body undergoes rapid oscillations while the rest of the body
      remains nearly at rest.
     Examples: prongs of a tuning fork, stretched guitar string, vibrating ruler.
