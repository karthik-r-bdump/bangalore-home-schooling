🗺️ Topographical Maps & Representation of Features

Topographical maps (survey sheets) are large-scale maps depicting natural and man-made features with high precision.

📐 Key Skills for Class 7​

  1. Grid System: Eastings (vertical lines numbered west to east) and Northings (horizontal lines numbered south to north).
  2. Reading Grid References:
    • Always read the Easting first, then the Northing ("Read East, then North").
  3. Contours:
    • Lines joining points of equal elevation above sea level.
    • Spacing indicates slope: Close contours = steep slope; distant contours = gentle slope.

 1. ANSWER THE FOLLOWING QUESTIONS

 a. What is a map?
     The primary source does not contain a single-sentence definition of a map, but it explains its nature
     and representation through the following exact verbatim statements:

     "The size of the map is much smaller than the actual size of the land that it represents. Due to this
     reduced size on a map, it is not possible to show the natural and man-made features on the map in
     their actual shape and size. Therefore, some symbols which are accepted all over the world, are
     used to represent features like rivers, roads, mountains, cities, etc on the map. These symbols are
     known as the conventional symbols. (Passage 10)"

     "...the scale helps us to reduce the size of the earth and represent it as a whole or in part on a piece
     of paper. (Passage 5)"


 b. Define topographical sheet.
     "In modern mapping, a topographic map or a topographical sheet is a type of map characterised by
     large-scale detail and quantitative representation of relief, usually using contour lines; topographic
     maps show both natural and man-made features also. (Passage 2)"


 c. Explain the term scale.
     "Scale is defined as the ratio of the distance between two points on the map and the corresponding
     distance on the ground. (Passage 1)"

 d. State the importance of a scale on a map.
     The scale's importance is defined in terms of representation and conversion in the sources:

     "Thus, the scale helps us to reduce the size of the earth and represent it as a whole or in part on a
     piece of paper. (Passage 5)"

     "The scale of a map shows how many smaller units such as centimetres or inches on the map
     represent how many kilometres or miles on the ground. (Passage 4)"


 e. Mention the various types of scales used on a map.
     "The scale used on the map is of three types: the Statement Scale, the Representative Fraction
     (RF) Scale and the Linear or the Graphic Scale. (Passage 1)"


 f. How does the RF represent the scale of a map?
     The Representative Fraction represents the scale of a map as a unit-free ratio:

     "The Representative Fraction or RF is the ratio between the distance or the map taken as unity and
     the corresponding distance on the ground. Beings ratio the RF has no units. For example, If the RF
     of a map is 1:50,000, then by definition it means 1 cm = 50,000 cm (Passage 5)"


 g. Describe how a linear scale is used in measuring the length of a part of a river.
     Measuring curved features like rivers uses the indirect or route distance method:

     "Indirect method or Route distance: This distance is measured along the road, railway line or river,
     etc. To measure this distance a piece of paper or thread is used. A thread or twine is twisted on the
     curves between the points taken for measurments. Then the distance is measured on the linear
     scale by stretching the thread or twine along the scale. (Passage 9)"


 h. Explain crowflight distance.
     "Direct or Crowfly method: It is measured as the crowfly by overlooking or avoiding the physical
     barries. Distance is measured by placing a strip of the paper or a foot-ruler or by pair of dividers on
     two needed points. Then the distance is measured from the linear scale. This is the distance in a
     Straight Line. (Passage 8)"
