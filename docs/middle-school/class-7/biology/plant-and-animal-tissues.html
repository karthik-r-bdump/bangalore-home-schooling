🔬 Plant and Animal Tissues

A tissue is a group of similar cells having a common origin and performing a specific function.

🌿 1. Plant Tissues​

  • Meristematic Tissue: Actively dividing cells with thin cellulose walls, dense cytoplasm, and prominent nuclei.
    • Apical Meristem: Tips of roots and stems (increases length).
    • Lateral Meristem (Cambium): Increases diameter/girth.
    • Intercalary Meristem: Base of nodes and internodes.
  • Permanent Tissue: Non-dividing cells adapted for specific functions.
    • Simple: Parenchyma (storage), Collenchyma (mechanical support & flexibility), Sclerenchyma (rigidity & strength).
    • Complex: Xylem (transports water and minerals unidirectionally) and Phloem (translocates food bidirectionally).

🐾 2. Animal Tissues​

  • Epithelial Tissue: Protective covering (Squamous, Cuboidal, Columnar, Ciliated).
  • Connective Tissue: Supports and binds (Blood, Bone, Cartilage, Areolar, Adipose, Tendons, Ligaments).
  • Muscular Tissue: Movement (Striated/Skeletal, Unstriated/Smooth, Cardiac).
  • Nervous Tissue: Composed of neurons for nerve impulse conduction.

     Introduction and Grounding Statement: This study guide compiles and solves the end-of-chapter
     exercises from Chapter 1 (Tissues) of the textbook. To ensure absolute academic integrity and verification,

 A. Name the Following
 1. Cell body of neuron.
    <b>Answer:</b> Cyton
      Verbatim Quote: "Cyton or cell body which contains a central nucleus." (Passage 50)

 2. Heart muscles.
    <b>Answer:</b> Cardiac muscles
      Verbatim Quote: "Cardiac Muscle (Specialised Muscle) : They are involuntary striated and non-fatigued muscle
      fibres which occur in the wall of the heart performing rhythmic contraction and relaxation continuously." (Passage
      46-47)

 3. Parenchyma cells containing chloroplast.
    <b>Answer:</b> Chlorenchyma
      Verbatim Quote: "Parenchyma cells in leaves contain chloroplast which helps in photosynthesis and are known
      as chlorenchyma." (Passage 12)




 B. Short Answer Questions
 1. Which tissue is present in the growing tips of roots and stem?
    <b>Answer:</b> Apical meristem (a type of Meristematic tissue) is present in the growing tips.
      Verbatim Quote: "Apical meristems : These are situated at the thin walled growing tips of roots, stems and
      branches and leads to elongation." (Passage 5)

 2. Name the tissue that is responsible for the transmission of stimulus. Draw its diagram also.
    <b>Answer:</b> Nervous tissue is responsible for transmitting stimuli and messages across the body. Its
    basic units are neurons or nerve cells.
      Verbatim Quote: "Nervous tissue is a highly specialised tissue which consists of basic units called neuron or
      nerve cells that transmit messages in our body." (Passage 50) "Nervous tissue: Tissue present in our brain and
      spinal cord which receives and sends signals (stimulus) in our body." (Passage 3) <b>Structural Breakdown
      (Diagram Reference):</b> According to the source, a neuron consists of: • <b>Cyton (cell body):</b> "...contains
      a central nucleus." • <b>Dendrites:</b> "The dendrons are short processes arising from the cyton and further
      branching into dendrites... they receive the impulses..." • <b>Axon:</b> "...is a single long cylindrical fibre that
      extends from the cyton... axon takes impulses away from the cell body." (Passage 50)

 3. Give the location and function of epithelial tissue.
    <b>Answer:</b> Epithelial tissue forms protective covers and linings across external and internal body
    chambers.
      Verbatim Quote (Location): "This tissue, forms a continuous sheet of cells that covers the external surface of the
      body like skin as well as lines the cavities of internal organs like mouth (cheek cells), stomach, intestine and wind
      pipe." (Passage 28) Verbatim Quote (Function): "It provides protection and prevents the entry of germs. It
      prevents the underlying cells from injury, entry of germs and from drying up (dessication)." (Passage 29)

 4. How are plant tissues classified? Give an outline of this classification.
    <b>Answer:</b> Plant tissues are classified into two primary categories: Meristematic (active cell dividers)
    and Permanent (mature, non-dividing cells). Permanent tissues are further sub-classified into Simple and
    Complex tissues.
      Verbatim Quote: "Plant tissues are of two types: Meristematic tissue and Permanent tissue. Permanent plant
      tissues are further of two types: Simple and Complex." (Passage 4) <b>Outline Classification of Plant
      Tissues:</b> • <b>PLANT TISSUES</b> ├── <b>Meristematic Tissues</b> (Rapid cell division, totipotency) │
      ├── Apical meristems │ ├── Lateral meristems │ └── Intercalary meristems └── <b>Permanent Tissues</b>
      (Mature cells incapable of division) ├── <b>Simple Tissues</b> (Single cell type: Parenchyma, Collenchyma,
      Sclerenchyma) └── <b>Complex Tissues</b> (Conducting tissues: Xylem, Phloem)
